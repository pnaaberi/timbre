A SMALL TOOL FOR BETTER FEEDBACK

Listen. Paint. Explain.

1. Compare your sounds

A is the reference, B the candidate to improve. Play, then press A or B to switch at the same absolute time. Loop replays your selected range, or the full active file. Different takes are not onset-aligned or time-stretched. RMS match attenuates the louder file; it is not LUFS normalization.

2. Paint a moment

Drag horizontally over a waveform to create a region. Click an existing colored region to edit it. Change and Keep notes have separate colors. Use Add by time for keyboard entry and millisecond precision. Waveforms show the maximum absolute amplitude across channels; they are not spectrograms.

3. Build a prompt

Save your notes, optionally add a goal and preference, then build the prompt. Your exact observations and desired changes are combined with filenames and timestamps. No automatic diagnosis, AI model, or audio rendering is involved. Attach the audio separately when sending the prompt to an editing tool.

Recorded demo samples

The online preset uses the VS Chamber Orchestra: Community Edition cello section, recorded by Sam Gossner & Simon Dalzell; sample cutting by Elan Hickler / Soundemote. Released under CC0 1.0.

A: susvib_A2_v1_1.wav — sustain with vibrato
B: trem_A2_v1_1.wav — tremolo

Same instrument section and source pitch label (A2), different bowed articulations. These are not a controlled before/after repair pair or a claim about one individual physical cello. Source files are unchanged.

Local by design

The two demo files are fetched from GitHub / jsDelivr. Imported files, waveform analysis, notes and prompt assembly stay in this browser. Notes may be saved in local browser storage; audio is not. Export a session to keep a portable copy, then reselect the original files when restoring it. Private browsing and file-URL storage policies can limit automatic saving.
